\subsection*{Problem 1}
The corresponding integral formulation of the governing equation is given by
\[
    \int_0^\ell \left[ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left(G I_p \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x} \right) \right] \delta\varphi \, dx = \int_0^\ell \rho I_p \ddot\varphi \, \delta\varphi \, dx \qquad \forall \, \delta\varphi,
\]
where \( \delta\varphi \) denotes the virtual rotation. Applying integration by parts to the left-hand side yields
\[
    -\int_0^\ell G I_p \left( \frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x} \right) \left( \frac{\partial\delta\varphi}{\partial x} \right) dx
    + \left[ G I_p \left( \frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x} \right) \delta\varphi \right]_0^\ell = \int_0^\ell \rho I_p \ddot\varphi \, \delta\varphi \, dx \qquad \forall \, \delta\varphi.
\]
By imposing natural boundary conditions that eliminate the boundary term, the expression simplifies to
\[
    \int_0^\ell G I_p \left( \frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x} \right) \left( \frac{\partial\delta\varphi}{\partial x} \right) dx +
    \int_0^\ell \rho I_p \ddot\varphi \, \delta\varphi \, dx = 0 \qquad \forall \, \delta\varphi.
\]
This yields the weak form of the problem: find \( \varphi \in \mathcal{U} \) such that
\[
    \int_0^\ell G I_p \left( \frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x} \right) \left( \frac{\partial\delta\varphi}{\partial x} \right) dx +
    \int_0^\ell \rho I_p \ddot\varphi \, \delta\varphi \, dx = 0 \qquad \forall \, \delta\varphi \in \mathcal{V},
\]
with the function spaces defined as
\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{U} & = \{ \varphi(\cdot,t) \in H^1(]0,\ell[)\ \forall t\in]0,T[ \}, \\
    \mathcal{V} & = \{\delta \varphi \in H^1(]0,\ell[)\}.
\end{align*}

The entries \( k_{ij} \) and \( m_{ij} \) of the elemental stiffness and mass matrices \( \mathbf{K} \) and \( \mathbf{M} \), respectively, are given by:
\begin{align*}
    k_{ij} & = \int_0^{\ell} G I_p \left( \frac{d h_i}{dx} \right) \left( \frac{d h_j}{dx} \right) dx, \\
    m_{ij} & = \int_0^{\ell} \rho I_p h_i h_j \, dx,
\end{align*}
where \(  h_1(x)=1-x/\ell \) and \(  h_2(x)=x/\ell\) are the standard linear shape functions defined over the interval \([0, \ell]\). For a two-node finite element of length \( \ell \), these integrals lead to the following matrices:
\[
    \mathbf{K} = \frac{G I_p}{\ell}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        1  & -1 \\
        -1 & 1
    \end{bmatrix} \quad\text{and}\quad
    \mathbf{M} = \frac{\rho\ell I_p}{6}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        2 & 1 \\
        1 & 2
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The corresponding Rayleigh quotient is expressed as
\[
    \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}) = \frac{\mathbf{w}^\mathrm{T} \mathbf{K} \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w}^\mathrm{T} \mathbf{M} \mathbf{w}} = \frac{3G}{\rho\ell^2} \frac{(w_1 - w_2)^2}{w_1^2 + w_1 w_2 + w_2^2},
\]
where \( \mathbf{w} = [w_1,\, w_2]^T \) denotes the vector of nodal values, either of the rotation field \( \varphi \) or the virtual rotation \( \delta\varphi \). To reduce the number of variables, we normalize by setting \( w_1 = 1 \), which is admissible since the Rayleigh quotient is homogeneous. This leads to the reduced form:
\[
    \mathcal{R}\left( \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ w_2 \end{bmatrix} \right) = \frac{3G}{\rho\ell^2} \frac{(1 - w_2)^2}{w_2^2 + w_2 + 1}.
\]

An approximation of the natural frequencies can be obtained by identifying the stationary points of \( \mathcal{R}(w_2) \). Solving
\[
    \frac{d \mathcal{R}}{dw_2} = \frac{3G}{\rho\ell^2} \left( \frac{2(w_2 - 1)}{w_2^2 + w_2 + 1} - \frac{(2w_2 + 1)(w_2 - 1)^2}{(w_2^2 + w_2 + 1)^2} \right) = 0,
\]
yields two critical points: \( w_2 = 1 \) and \( w_2 = -1 \), corresponding to the Rayleigh quotient values:
\[
    \mathcal{R}\left( \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right) = 0, \qquad \mathcal{R}\left( \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \right) = \frac{12G}{\rho\ell^2}.
\]

The vanishing of the first computed frequency is consistent with the presence of a rigid-body rotation mode in a free-free torsional shaft. This result confirms that the formulation correctly captures the underlying physical behavior, including the absence of restoring torque for uniform rotation. The second frequency provides an estimate of the first non-zero torsional natural frequency:
\[
    \omega_2^{\text{approx}} = \frac{\sqrt{12}}{\ell} \sqrt{\frac{G}{\rho}},
\]
to be compared with the exact value for a free-free shaft:
\[
    \omega_2^{\text{exact}} = \frac{\pi}{\ell} \sqrt{\frac{G}{\rho}}.
\]
The relative error between the approximate and exact values is given by
\[
    \text{Relative error} = \left| \frac{\sqrt{12} - \pi}{\pi} \right| \approx 0.102,
\]
which corresponds to approximately 10.2\%. This level of accuracy is typical for low-order finite element approximations and validates the adopted formulation and shape functions.

\subsection*{Problem 2}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For the one element discretization of length $h$, as done in Problem 1, the two linear shape functions have derivatives $-1/h$ and $1/h$. Integration of the stiffness and inertia terms gives
          \[
              {}^e\vct K=\frac{GI_p}{h}\colv{1&-1\\-1&1},
              \qquad  {}^e\vct M=\frac{\rho I_ph}{6}\colv{2&1\\1&2}.
          \]
          Each interior node belongs to two elements. Each free end belongs to one. Assembling and scaling with $h=\ell/4$ gives
          \[
              \bK=4\colv{1&-1&0&0&0\\-1&2&-1&0&0\\0&-1&2&-1&0\\
                  0&0&-1&2&-1\\0&0&0&-1&1},\qquad
              \bM=\frac1{24}\colv{2&1&0&0&0\\1&4&1&0&0\\0&1&4&1&0\\
                  0&0&1&4&1\\0&0&0&1&2}.
          \]
          Every row of $\bK$ sums to zero. A rigid rotation therefore has zero strain energy and zero frequency.

          To obtain the eigenpairs in a closed-form, set $\theta_k=k\pi/4$, $c_k=\cos\theta_k$ and try $p^{(k)}_j=\cos(j\theta_k)$, $j=0,\dots,4$ (node index $j$). Using $p_{j-1}+p_{j+1}=2c_k\,p_j$:
          \begin{itemize}
              \item interior rows $j=1,2,3$: $4(2-2c_k)p_j=\dfrac{\Lambda}{24}(4+2c_k)p_j$;
              \item left end row: $4(1-c_k)=\dfrac{\Lambda}{24}(2+c_k)$, the same relation;
              \item right end row: since $p_{4-j}=(-1)^kp_j$, it is the mirror image of the left one.
          \end{itemize}
          All rows are therefore satisfied by
          \[
              \Lambda_k^{\rm FE}=96\,\frac{1-\cos\theta_k}{2+\cos\theta_k},
              \qquad \Omega_k^{\rm FE}=\sqrt{\Lambda_k^{\rm FE}},\qquad k=0,\ldots,4 .
          \]

          The continuum problem $GI_p\varphi''+\rho I_p\omega^2\varphi=0$, $\varphi'(0)=\varphi'(\ell)=0$ gives $\Omega_k=k\pi$ and $\varphi_k=\cos(k\pi x/\ell)$. At the nodes $x_j=j\ell/4$ one has $\varphi_k(x_j)=\cos(jk\pi/4)=p_j^{(k)}$: the five-node FE eigenvectors coincide with the samples of the exact modes, so $\mathrm{MAC}_{\bM}=1$ for all $k$. Between nodes the FE shape is piecewise linear. The frequencies, on the other hand, are all overestimated (a Ritz-type property of the consistent mass model), and the error grows with $k$ because the mode has fewer elements per half-wave: $2.6\%$ for $k=1$ (four elements per half-wave) against $19.5\%$ for $k=3$. Only the lowest elastic mode is reasonably resolved by this mesh.
    \item For $\vct p=\vct R\vct p^*$ stationarity of the Rayleigh quotient gives $\vct R\T(\bK\vct R\vct p^*-\Lambda^*\bM\vct R\vct p^*)=\vct0$, i.e.\ the $2\times2$ problem $\vct K^*\vct p^*=\Lambda^*\vct M^*\vct p^*$. Write $\vct R=[\vct r_1\ \vct r_2]$ with $\vct r_1=(1,1,1,1,1)\T$ and $\vct r_2=(16,11,0,-11,-16)\T$. Then:
          \begin{itemize}
              \item $K^*_{11}=K^*_{12}=0$ because $\bK\vct r_1=\vct0$;
              \item $K^*_{22}=4\left[(16-11)^2+(11-0)^2+(0+11)^2+(-11+16)^2\right]=4\cdot292=1168$;
              \item $M^*_{11}=\vct r_1\T\bM\vct r_1=1$ (the total dimensionless mass), and $\vct r_1\T\bM=(1,2,2,2,1)/8$ gives $M^*_{12}=\tfrac18(16+22-22-16)=0$;
              \item $M^*_{22}=\frac1{24}\sum_{\rm elements}2(a^2+ab+b^2)=\frac{2696}{24}=\frac{337}{3}$.
          \end{itemize}
          Hence
          \[
              \vct K^*=\colv{0&0\\0&1168},\qquad
              \vct M^*=\colv{1&0\\0&337/3}.

          Thus $\Lambda^*_0=0$, and $\Lambda^*_1=\frac{3504}{337}\approx10.397626$ with reduced eigenvectors $\vct p_0^*=(1,0)\T$ and $\vct p_1^*=(0,1)\T$, so $\Omega_1^*=3.224535$. Recovering $\vct p=\vct R\vct p^*$ and scaling to unit left-end amplitude,

              \vct p_0=(1,1,1,1,1)\T,\qquad
              \vct p_1=(1,\tfrac{11}{16},0,-\tfrac{11}{16},-1)\T .
          \]
          The second column is antisymmetric about the midpoint and has zero slope at both ends, so it is a good approximation of $\cos(\pi x)$; the constant column reproduces the rigid mode exactly.

    \item Now $\vct R=[\vct r_1\ \vct r_2]$ with $\vct r_1=(2,-1,0,-1,2)\T$ and $\vct r_2=(0,1,-2,1,0)\T$. Direct evaluation gives
          \[
              \begin{aligned}
                   & \vct r_1\T\bK\vct r_1=\vct r_2\T\bK\vct r_2=4\cdot20=80 \\
                   & \vct r_1\T\bK\vct r_2=4(-12)=-48                        \\
                   & \vct r_1\T\bM\vct r_1=\vct r_2\T\bM\vct r_2=\tfrac23    \\
                   & \vct r_1\T\bM\vct r_2=0
              \end{aligned}
          \]
          So
          \[
              \vct K^*=\colv{80&-48\\-48&80},\qquad
              \vct M^*=\tfrac23\colv{1&0\\1&1}.
          \]
          Thus the Ritz pairs are
          $$\Lambda^*_1=48,\ \vct p^*_1=(1,1)\T\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad \Lambda^*_2=192,\ \vct p^*_2=(1,-1)\T.$$
          The recovered shapes, scaled at the left end, are $\mathbf p_0=(1,0,-1,0,1)\T$ and $\mathbf p_0=(1,-1,1,-1,1)\T$.

          Which modes are returned ? Computing $\mathrm{MAC}_{\bM}$ against all five FE modes gives $1$ for $k=2$ (first pair) and $k=4$ (second pair) and $0$ for the others. The reason is structural:
          \begin{itemize}
              \item both columns are symmetric about the midpoint, whereas the first elastic mode is antisymmetric: $\mathrm{MAC}_{\bM}$ with $k=1$ (and $k=3$) is zero;
              \item $\vct1\T\bM\vct R=\vct0$, since $\vct1\T\bM=(1,2,2,2,1)/8$ and both columns have zero weighted mean: the space is mass-orthogonal to the rigid mode, so $\mathrm{MAC}_{\bM}=0$ with $k=0$.
          \end{itemize}
          Symmetric vectors that are $\bM$-orthogonal to $\vct1$ form exactly $\mathrm{span}\{\vct p_2,\vct p_4\}$, a two-dimensional invariant subspace of the pencil, so the Rayleigh--Ritz procedure returns $k=2,4$ \emph{exactly}.

The mode identification is provided by $\mathrm{MAC}_{\bM}$, which is why it is part of the error check.
